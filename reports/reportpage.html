Deployability

Every per-period figure on this page is a mean over overlapping forecast periods, and a portfolio cannot hold an overlapping period. The strategy signals daily and holds five days, so what it trades is five overlapping tranches: one fifth of capital rebalances into each day's targets. That construction holds every phase at once and has no free parameters.

What the columns mean. Active is the compounded daily active return: the product over every trading day of 1 + (strategy return − benchmark return). It is not strategy equity divided by benchmark equity, which is why that ratio is shown beside it — compounding a difference of returns is not the same as taking a ratio of compounded returns, and the gap between the two columns is the covariance of the two legs. Strategy drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough fall of the compounded strategy equity curve, a true percentage of capital. Active drawdown is the same measure on the compounded active-return curve. Neither is the summed-return drawdown reported under Portfolio backtest below.

Why the training window is 63 dates

Five window sizes were measured on an identical set of 223 evaluation dates. The relationship is an inverted U, not monotonic: Rank IC peaks at 21 dates and collapses to roughly zero at 252, which is what one would expect if long windows average across market regimes that no longer apply.

63 was chosen over the Rank-IC-optimal 21 because it has the best top-5 hit rate and the only clearly positive top-5 return, and portfolio construction selects a top-N basket rather than trading the full ranking.

The sweep figure is not the headline figure. This table reports Rank IC 0.028126 for the chosen 63-date window while the headline reports 0.033360. Both are gradient boosting on the 5-day horizon and neither is wrong. The sweep scores 223 test dates, subsampled at every tenth date and starting late enough that the 252-date window is also reachable, so that all five configurations are scored on an identical set of dates. Production walk-forward scores every date it can, 2,418 of them. A shorter, later, sparser subsample is a different sample of the same estimator, so the two figures are not required to agree.

Chart and table render from the same rows of training_window_diagnostic.csv — the chart rounds to four decimals, the table prints six, and neither carries a separate copy of the numbers. Train rows is the measured count of dataset rows falling on the first window's training dates, not the window length times 30: 126 dates measures 3,779 rather than 3,780 (1 short); 252 dates measures 7,555 rather than 7,560 (5 short). Ten dates in the dataset carry 29 constituents rather than 30 because FLC has no data for the week of 2017-01-16 and ROS none for the week of 2021-01-25, and a window spanning one of those dates is one row short for each. The shortfall is a data gap, not a counting error.

What the before column is. It was captured under train_size = 3 and under the cost model as it stood before commit bfdabcf, which charged commission and slippage on one leg of a trade instead of two and omitted the statutory transfer tax, pricing a round trip at 0.2 percent where the paper account charges 0.5. A before/after pair in an after-cost column therefore straddles two changes at once and is not the effect of the training window. The Rank IC columns do isolate the training window, because Rank IC is a rank correlation and does not depend on costs at all — which is why those columns are identical to six decimals before and after the cost correction.

Overlap disclosure: the 10-day horizon’s 2,403 evaluated dates correspond to roughly 240 genuinely non-overlapping periods. Average after-cost return is per forecast period, not annualized.

Across columns they are not: the before column covers 2,478 dates and the after column 2,418, because a 63-date training window needs 60 more dates of warm-up than a 3-date window before it can predict, and those dates drop out of the evaluation.

Is any of this distinguishable from zero? sector_diversified + normal has mean active return 0.000225 with a 95% interval of [−0.000624, 0.001085], which spans zero. normal + price_limit_aware has mean active return −0.000822 with a 95% interval of [−0.002056, 0.000462], which spans zero. The interval is a 95 percent moving-block bootstrap on the mean after-cost active return, 2,000 resamples, blocked at the 5-day forecast horizon so that the overlap between consecutive period returns stays inside a block. An interval that spans zero is the reason no alpha claim is made here; it is not a claim that the mean is zero.

Cumulative figures in this table sum overlapping forecast-period active returns, so they are not compounded portfolio returns, and max_active_drawdown in benchmark_results.csv is a running-peak fall on that same summed series — in summed-return units, not as a percentage of capital. The Deployability table at the top of the page uses the other convention: both of its drawdown columns are peak-to-trough falls of a compounded equity curve and are genuine percentages of capital. The two conventions share no number and should not be compared.

Why 60 rows a date is not 60 duplicate rows. The VN30 has 30 constituents on any date, and this file stacks two models, gradient_boosting and random_forest, in one table with a model_name column. A fully covered date therefore carries 30 × 2 = 60 rows, and 208 × 60 = 12,480. Each (date, ticker) pair appears twice by design and each (date, ticker, model) key once.

Why 54 tickers and not the dataset’s 60. The modeling dataset covers 60 tickers across the full history — the 30 current constituents plus 30 former ones. This file covers the 54 of those 60 that were VN30 members on at least one date inside its own range. The six missing names (FLC, HAG, HCM, HNG, ITA, PPC) all left the index before 2017-08-09, the first date a prediction can be made after the 252-date training warm-up, so under point-in-time membership they correctly never appear. 60 is the dataset-wide ticker count; 54 is this file's; 30 is the count on any single date.

Which period is which. 2017-08-09 to 2026-07-01 is this prediction file's range, which is shorter than the backtest window in Deployability at the top of the page: it starts after a 252-date warm-up and stops where a 10-day forward label stops being observable. The signal date in Latest diagnostics is the last date in the dataset and is later than both, because a signal needs no realized forward label. The three periods are reported separately and no figure is carried between them.

Re-derived from data/processed/paper_horizon_predictions.parquet on this run: 12,480 rows, 208 dates, 60 rows per date, 54 distinct tickers, 2 models (gradient_boosting, random_forest). What did not improve

  • A one-feature momentum baseline still beats every trained model — 0.002264 against 0.001992. Narrowed, unresolved.
  • The 10-day horizon Rank IC fell from 0.039083 to 0.037407.
  • Margins remain thin. The most conservative portfolio configuration does not clear cost, at a diagnostic Sharpe of −0.042, and a 95% bootstrap interval on mean active return of [−0.002056, 0.000462], which spans zero.
  • Concentration still reduces performance, which is what a weak ranking signal looks like.
  • Foreign buy/sell features remain unpromoted. Pre-registered test, paired block bootstrap on daily Rank IC over 222 non-overlapping signals: mean difference −0.002608, 95% CI [−0.013314, 0.008297]. The interval spans zero and the point estimate is negative. The promotion rule was fixed before the result was seen: promote only if the interval excludes zero.
Multiple comparisons. The training window was chosen by measuring five values against the same history, on top of three horizons, five ablation configurations, four optimizer modes, two execution modes and five foreign-flow feature sets already tested against it. The improvement is consistent across horizons, ablations and portfolio modes, which makes selection noise unlikely — but nothing here corrects for the number of comparisons, and the intervals that do exist (12 strategy and baseline rows of benchmark_results.csv, plus the foreign-flow test) are each marginal rather than family-wise. Rank IC, hit rate, and every per-horizon and per-ablation figure above is a point estimate with no interval at all.
